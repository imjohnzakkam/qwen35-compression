Where Blaze stands

The idea holds: a 4-bit Qwen3.5 checkpoint wastes a quarter of its bytes on vocabulary rows it rarely uses. Moving those bytes is free, and spending them on a 6-bit body brings the 4B to BF16's level at a standard 4-bit file's size. As of 17 October all of it serves on stock vLLM, using vLLM's own 6-bit kernel.

Seventeen A30 runs, 15–30 October 2026 · Qwen3.5-4B and 2B · instruct mode, greedy decoding · full research page · experiment board · scorecard
Proven

The vocabulary ladder costs nothing (0.0002 excess loss). At W4A16's 3.78 GB, a 6-bit Blaze-Q drifts 8.5× less than Glaze v2 and is within a point of BF16 on 12 of 13 tasks.

Ships today

Glaze v2, GPTQ and AutoRound with the ladder: 23% smaller (2.91–2.92 GB) at unchanged quality, 16% faster for one user. Blaze-S on AutoRound's body: 2.60 GB at AutoRound's quality, 174 tok/s, 2.74 GB of GPU memory against W4A16's 3.92. Blaze-S scores at or above AutoRound on MMLU-Pro, MATH-500 and IFEval, and leaves 11% more KV cache than GPTQ W4A16. The 2B Blaze-Q matches BF16 at W4A16's size, served for real.

Unblocked

The real 4B Blaze-Q, 385 KB under GPTQ W4A16's file, runs on vLLM 0.29's bundled 6-bit kernel with the same 0.0017 drift, at 147 tokens a second for one user against GPTQ's 151, in GPTQ's 3.92 GB of GPU memory. No custom kernel was needed. Rebuilt on a 30-language census at the same size, it drifts 0.0023 on 11 languages (Thai 0.0070) against GPTQ's 0.0655, and matches BF16 on MGSM in 10 other languages (65.2) where GPTQ drops to 59.0.

Spent

₹881.31 (about $9.15) of GPU time across every gate, the full suite and eleven follow-ups, including one run that overshot its cap by ₹15.67 and one stopped by a driver bug after its pilot (₹20.76). ₹414.38 left; nothing is running.

Quality, against BF16

Every model here is a Qwen3.5-4B checkpoint scored on the same questions as BF16. Three axes are benchmarks; two measure drift on BF16's own 1,501 answers: how often the model would pick a different next token, and how likely it finds BF16's tokens. The ladder add-on versions overlap their sources, so they are not drawn separately.

Size, against drift

Down is better quality, left is a smaller file. The ladder moves every published checkpoint left by 0.86 GB without moving it up. The 6-bit Blaze-Q is the lowest point at the standard size, and since 17 October it serves too.

What is in the way

  • The English census. The ladder's 8-bit rows hold 26.5% of non-English tokens, and Blaze-Q's drift on Thai is 0.028 (GPTQ W4A16: 0.109). A census with FineWeb-2 in 30 languages, at the same size, cuts the ladder's multilingual cost 2.6× and Thai's 9×. Fixed on 29 October: Blaze-Q rebuilt on it drifts 0.0023 on other languages (was 0.0051) and 0.0070 on Thai (was 0.028), for +0.00008 on English.
  • Blaze's own 4-bit GPTQ. It barely beats plain rounding at 4 bits, which is why Blaze-S lost accuracy. Reusing the published bodies, as the add-on does, sidesteps it.
  • One MathVista point. The full suite missed its 1-point rule on MathVista alone (−1.1, interval −2.6 to +0.5), within noise.
