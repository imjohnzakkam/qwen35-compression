Blaze-Q and Blaze-S

One method, two Qwen3.5-4B checkpoints. Blaze-Q spends the vocabulary's idle bytes on a 6-bit body and reaches BF16's level at a standard 4-bit file's size. Blaze-S banks them instead: 31% smaller than a 4-bit export, at AutoRound's quality. Both serve on stock vLLM 0.29 with a small plugin.

Seventeen A30 runs, 15–30 October 2026 · instruct mode, greedy decoding · research page · where Blaze stands · scorecard
3.78 GB · same size as GPTQ W4A16

Blaze-Q

A 6-bit GPTQ body with 23 of 64 units at 8 bits, the multilingual vocabulary ladder and an 8-bit vision tower. Drift 0.0018, within a point of BF16 on 12 of 13 tasks, BF16's scores on code and 10 other languages, 147 tokens a second.

2.60 GB · 31% smaller

Blaze-S

AutoRound's published 4-bit body, copied as is, with the multilingual ladder and an 8-bit vision tower. At or above AutoRound on every panel task, 175 tokens a second against GPTQ's 151, 11% more KV cache.

Quality, against BF16

Six measures on the same questions for every checkpoint, each scaled to BF16's own score. Token agreement is how often the model's next-token choice matches BF16's along BF16's 1,501 benchmark answers.

What the numbers say

  • Blaze-Q is BF16's quality in a 4-bit file's bytes. Excess loss 0.0018 against GPTQ's 0.054 at the same 3.78 GB, and BF16's scores where GPTQ loses most: MATH-500 (83.0 against 75.8) and MGSM in 10 other languages (65.2, equal to BF16, against 59.0; paired change 0.0, interval −3.2 to +3.0).
  • Blaze-S is a 4-bit model's quality in 31% fewer bytes. Its body is AutoRound's, and it scores at or above AutoRound on every panel task (MMLU-Pro +1.0, MATH-500 +3.2, IFEval 0.0). It is the fastest for one user and leaves the most room for long requests.
  • Long batches stay compute-bound. 64 prompts of 16k tokens take about 130 s on every 4- and 6-bit checkpoint against 114 s in BF16; the extra cache buys concurrency, not batch speed.

Serving them

Both are real checkpoints in the compressed-tensors format, with the vocabulary stored as an 8-bit block of the 65,536 busiest rows, a 4-bit block of the rest, and an index map. The blaze-vllm plugin teaches vLLM 0.29's Qwen3.5 model to read that table: lookups come from the LM head's own Marlin copy, so the table is held once on the GPU. Blaze-Q's 6-bit groups run on vLLM's bundled Humming kernel, which needs the CUDA NVRTC builtins on the library path; the plugin can instead run them as 8-bit for GPUs without it (same drift, 4.93 GB, 126 tokens a second).

Status

  • Not published yet. The plan is a separate Blaze collection on Hugging Face: Qwen3.5-4B-Blaze-Q and Qwen3.5-4B-Blaze-S, with the plugin bundled in each repo and on PyPI, private first.
  • Blaze-Q's checkpoint is built and downloaded (3,780,277,088 bytes). Blaze-S is rebuilt from AutoRound's published export without a GPU (2,598,000,440 bytes when measured).
  • Next measured: a smoke test of the published repos from the Hub. The 2B Blaze-Q follows later, and the 9B is on hold.
